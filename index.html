Method & formulas

Geometry. Vehicle frame: x forward, y left, z up. Lamps at (0, ±s/2, hlamp), observer eye at (d, ±offset, heye), with offset to the left for RHT and to the right for LHT. The direction to the eye is rotated into the lamp frame (aim) and converted to the file's angle system: Type A H = atan2(y,x), V = asin(z/r); Type B V = atan2(z,x), H = asin(y/r); Type C γ = acos(−z/r), C = atan2(y,x). Intensity uses bilinear interpolation with IES symmetry handling.

Eye illuminance. E = τ · I / r² per lamp, with the eye plane either normal to the lamp (worst case) or normal to the line of sight (× cos θ), summed over both lamps.

Glare angle. θ is the angle between the oncoming driver's line of sight (straight along the road) and the direction to each lamp, clamped at the set minimum.

de Boer (Schmidt-Clausen & Bindels). W = 5 − 2·log₁₀ Σ Ei / [Cp00(1 + √(La/CpL))·θi0.46], with Cp00 = 0.003 lx·min−0.46, CpL = 0.04 cd/m², θ in arcmin. Displayed W is clipped to 1–9.

Veiling luminance. CIE 146: Lv = Σ Ei[10/θ³ + (5/θ² + 0.1p/θ)(1 + (A/62.5)⁴) + 0.0025p], θ in degrees. Stiles–Holladay: Lv = 10 Σ Ei/θi². TI = 65·Lv/La0.8 % (defined for La ≤ 5 cd/m²).

Validity. CIE 146 veiling luminance is defined for θ = 0.1–100°, Stiles–Holladay for about 1–30°; de Boer data cover roughly 0.1–15°. Points outside these ranges are marked in the table and CSV. At long distances θ becomes small and the 1/θ³ term in CIE 146 dominates, so TI can grow with distance; read those values with care.

Scope. Straight, flat road; static observer looking along the road; no dirty-glass scatter. Planned next: curves, gradients, mirror glare for preceding vehicles, and a PDF report.
